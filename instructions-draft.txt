INTRO (ALWAYS VISIBLE)

Play chess on Lichess by speaking. Your opponent's moves are spoken aloud, so you can play at a real board, or across the room, without looking at a screen.

Three steps: sign in below, tap Start, then say your moves — "e4", "knight f3", "castle kingside". Everything else is detail, in the sections below.

1. SIGNING IN

Signing in happens on lichess.org, so this site never sees your password. Lichess will ask you to allow this site to do 3 things on your behalf: read challenges, send and accept challenges, and play games. You can revoke this site's access any time under Preferences → Security on Lichess, or by signing out here.

Sign in once and the page waits for a game — one started here, in the Lichess app, or by a friend's challenge.

2. STARTING

Tap Start once. iOS will not open a microphone without a real tap, so this is required every visit; the page will ask for microphone permission the first time.

Start controls the voice only. Signing in is what connects you to Lichess, so a game keeps running even with the voice off.

The button says where it is:

- Start — before you tap.
- Listening — the microphone is live.
- On — the voice is running, with the microphone paused for a moment while it speaks.

3. SAYING A MOVE

Say it the way you would say it to a person:

- "e4" or "echo four" — letters can be plain or NATO.
- "knight f3", "bishop takes e5", "queen d5"
- "castle" on its own, or "castle kingside" / "castle queenside"
- "c1 equals bishop" to promote — any piece, not just a queen.
- "knight b d7" when two of the same piece can reach a square.

Saying "push" before a pawn move — "push c4" — is worth the habit: it names the piece, which skips a question.

Saying "check" or "checkmate" at the end is fine, and simply ignored.

4. OTHER THINGS YOU CAN SAY

- "repeat" — say the last move again.
- "clock" — speak both clocks.
- "whose turn" — whose move it is.
- "what is on f3" — the piece on a square.
- "where are my knights" — every square a piece stands on.
- "resign", or "offer a draw" — each asks yes or no first.
- "memo …" — anything after the word "memo" is written to the log, for reporting a problem later.
- "flip clock" — swap which side of the clock screen is yours.

5. WHEN IT ISN'T SURE

It asks, and the answer is always one word.

"Did you mean e4? Yes or no."
Say yes or no. Saying no walks through the other pieces that could go there, and naming a piece — "knight" — jumps straight to it.

"I heard bishop bravo. Say the rank."
Say "four". It heard half a square and wants the other half.

"Cancel"
Drops any question, so you can start the move over.

"That's not a legal move. Say again."
It understood you, but the move is not legal in this position.

6. THE BUTTONS

- Start — microphone and speech on or off.
- Settings — the switches described below.
- Clock — a black screen showing just the two clocks, with the side to move drawn heavier. It keeps the screen awake. Tap it again to come back.
- Log — everything heard and done, newest last. Copy puts it on the clipboard, which is the useful thing to send if something goes wrong.
- Practice — understands moves and answers with a random legal reply, sending nothing to Lichess. Good for trying phrasings.

7. SETTINGS

All modes

- Confirm my move — off by default. On, every move is read back for a yes before it is sent. Slower, but nothing reaches Lichess unheard.
- Guard pawn pushes — on by default. A bare square like "e4" means a pawn push, but it is also what a dropped piece name sounds like. When a piece could legally reach that square too, it asks first. Naming the piece — "push e4", "pawn e4" — skips the question.

Voice mode

- Speak my move — your own move read back once Lichess accepts it. Your opponent's move is always spoken.

Clock mode

- Speak my move — the same choice while the clock screen is up, kept separate because the move row can do the confirming there.
- Speak opponent's move — off if you would rather read it than hear it.
- Show moves — the move row on the clock screen. Turning it off makes the clock digits larger.
- Speak messages and Show messages — where everything that is not a move goes: questions, errors, game over. One of the two always stays on, so a question can never go unanswered in silence.

8. A BETTER VOICE

The page speaks with your system voice. On an iPhone or iPad a much better one is free: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → English, then pick a Premium or Enhanced voice (Ava is a good one) and let it download.

Re-download it after an iOS update if the sound gets worse.
